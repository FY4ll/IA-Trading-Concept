## Q1. Turn-of-the-month (TOM) effect and the "dash for cash" (Ariel 1987; Lakonishok & Smidt 1988; McConnell & Xu 2008; Etula, Rinne, Suominen & Vaittinen 2020) — equities and FX; still present?

### Takeaway
The TOM effect is one of the oldest and best-documented equity calendar regularities, and Etula et al. give it a mechanism (institutional month-end cash needs around the last settlement day, T-3: prices fall into T-3 and rebound afterwards). But evidence on post-publication survival is split: several studies say it vanished or shrank in the S&P 500 cash market after ~1990-2001, while others find it persists (in narrower form) in S&P 500 futures/ETFs. For a CFD trader it is at best a small, few-days, index-only tilt (a few tenths of a percent per month), not a stand-alone edge; FX evidence for a TOM effect is indirect (month-end fix/hedging flows, see Q5).

### Cited Findings
- Original findings (FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE — NOT RE-VERIFIED): Ariel (1987, Journal of Financial Economics 18(1):161-174, "A monthly effect in stock returns") found US index returns positive only in the first half of the trading month (last day of prior month through mid-month), ~zero in the second half; Lakonishok & Smidt (1988, Review of Financial Studies 1(4):403-425, 90 years of DJIA) found the 4-day window from the last trading day to the third day of the new month earned ~0.47% vs ~0.06% for an average 4-day period; McConnell & Xu (2008, Financial Analysts Journal 64(2):49-64, "Equity returns at the turn of the month") found that over 1926-2005 essentially all of the positive US market return accrued in the 4-day TOM window (day -1 to +3) and that the effect appeared in 30 of 35 countries. — [McConnell & Xu, FAJ via DOI 10.2469/faj.v64.n2.11](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v64.n2.11); [Lakonishok & Smidt, RFS via DOI 10.1093/rfs/1.4.403](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/1.4.403); [Ariel, JFE via DOI 10.1016/0304-405X(87)90066-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(87)90066-3)
- "Dash for Cash" (working-paper version, Etula, Rinne, Suominen & Vaittinen): strong return reversals in the US value-weighted index around the last monthly settlement day, T-3, which guarantees liquidity for month-end cash distributions; similar reversals internationally; stronger in countries with large mutual-fund ownership; in the US they became stronger over time as mutual-fund ownership rose; direct trade-level evidence that institutional trading contributes; stronger for stocks more held by mutual funds and for liquid stocks (VERIFIED, this session, from abstract). — [Dash for Cash PDF (orbilu.uni.lu)](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/21145/1/Dash4Cash.pdf); [Hedge Fund Alpha summary](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/stock-returns-month-end-liquidity-needs/)
- Practitioner summary of the same paper: since 1926, holding the S&P 500 only ~7 business days around month-end captured "almost the entire market return with forty percent lower volatility"; "since 1987, all of the positive equity returns have accrued during these seven trading days, and the average returns during the rest of the month have been negative" (VERIFIED as what the summary says; the primary RFS text was not opened). — [Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/stock-returns-month-end-liquidity-needs/)
- Published version: Review of Financial Studies (2020) 33(1):75-111, "Dash for Cash: Monthly Market Impact of Institutional Liquidity Needs" (FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE — NOT RE-VERIFIED; volume/pages and any FX/Treasury results in the published version could not be opened). — [RFS via DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhz041](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhz041)
- Decay evidence: a Finance Research Letters paper (2024/25) titled "The disappearing turn-of-month effect" reports the TOM effect is no longer significant after 2001 and attributes this to improved liquidity after decimalization (arbitrageurs can trade against it; investors no longer bunch trades at month-end) (VERIFIED from search snippet only; full text 403). — [ScienceDirect: The disappearing turn-of-month effect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612324014909)
- Earlier decay evidence: TOM effects in S&P 500 futures reported to disappear after 1990, carrying over to the spot market (Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta working paper 2000-11, VERIFIED snippet). — [IDEAS: Atlanta Fed WP 2000-11](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fip/fedawp/2000-11.html)
- Counter-evidence: TOM returns remain significantly higher than non-TOM returns for the S&P 500 and its ETF, though the window shrank after ETF trading began (Financial Planning Association journal, "The Turn-of-the-Month Anomaly in the Age of ETFs"); and a study of S&P 500 index futures reports TOM as "the only calendar effect that is statistically and economically significant and persistent over time" (VERIFIED snippets). — [FPA Journal](https://www.financialplanningassociation.org/article/journal/APR11-turn-month-anomaly-age-etfs-reexamination-return-enhancement-strategies); [Paperswithbacktest summary of "Closing the Question on the Continuation of TOM Effects" (S&P futures)](https://paperswithbacktest.com/strategies/closing-the-question-on-the-continuation-of-turn-of-the-month-effects-evidence-from-the-s-p-index-futures-contract)
- Retail-media distortion: blogs/newsletters (e.g., ETF Trends, Quantpedia strategy pages, trading newsletters) typically present TOM as a ready-made strategy using full-sample (since 1926 or 1987) averages, without splitting pre/post-publication or post-2001, and without CFD costs. — [ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/etf-strategist-content-hub/turn-month-effect/); [Quantpedia TOM strategy page](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/turn-of-the-month-in-equity-indexes)

### Inferences
- Horizon fits COLLAPSAR (days -4 to +3 around month-end), markets = equity indices (US500, US30, GER40, etc.). Expected gross effect is of the order of a few tenths of a percent per month concentrated in ~4-7 days; with index CFD spreads small, costs are not the binding problem — statistical fragility and decay are.
- Because Etula et al.'s mechanism (pension/mutual fund cash needs at T-3) is institutional and structural, it is more plausible to persist than a "pure" calendar anomaly, but the decay papers show it can be competed away; any use should be as an input feature (calendar position within month, distance to T-3), not a rule — consistent with the project's "information, not trigger" principle.
- Weekend constraint: TOM windows often span a weekend; holding through it is forbidden here, which cuts part of the window.

### Gaps
- Could not open the published RFS (2020) text to confirm whether Etula et al. report FX or Treasury month-end effects and the exact basis-point size around T-3 (paywall/certificate error).
- Authors and exact sample/numbers of the 2024/25 FRL "disappearing TOM" paper not retrieved (403).
- No peer-reviewed post-2015 evidence found specifically for TOM in gold, oil or crypto.

## Q2. Pre-FOMC announcement drift (Lucca & Moench 2015) and the macro-announcement premium (Savor & Wilson 2013) — has it disappeared?

### Takeaway
Lucca & Moench documented ~0.5% S&P 500 gains in the 24 hours before scheduled FOMC announcements (1994-2011). After publication it essentially disappeared: Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert (2021) find no drift over 2016-2019 with or without press conferences; a 0DTE-options paper attributes its disappearance to the new ability to hedge event risk. The broader "announcement-day premium" (Savor & Wilson 2013: 11.4 bp vs 1.1 bp) is a risk-premium story with weaker post-publication evidence. Treat both as dead or very weak for trading purposes.

### Cited Findings
- Lucca & Moench (2015, Journal of Finance 70(1):329-371): US stocks earned on average about 0.5% in the 24 hours preceding scheduled FOMC announcements, 1994-March 2011 (VERIFIED via search summary). — [Lucca & Moench, Fed NY Staff Report 512 (citations page)](https://ideas.repec.org/r/fip/fednsr/512.html); [ResearchGate: The Pre-FOMC announcement drift](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228316087_The_Pre-FOMC_announcement_drift)
- (FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE — NOT RE-VERIFIED) In the original paper the drift (~49 bp) accounted for roughly 80% of the annual realized US equity premium since 1994, appeared in other major equity indices, and was not found in Treasuries, FX or commodities in a comparable way. — [Lucca & Moench JF via DOI 10.1111/jofi.12196](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12196)
- Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert (2021, Finance Research Letters vol. 40), "The Disappearing Pre-FOMC Announcement Drift": after first appearing before FOMC announcements with press conferences, the drift "essentially disappeared after 2015", both with and without press conferences, over Jan 2016-Dec 2019; attributed to reduced uncertainty (VIX) (VERIFIED). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1544612320315956); [SSRN 3134546](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3134546); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finlet:v:40:y:2021:i:c:s1544612320315956)
- Song (AFA paper, "Pre-FOMC Uncertainty Accumulation: Evidence from 0DTE Options"): introduction of Wednesday-expiring 0DTE options made the pre-announcement drift disappear, interpreted as the drift having been compensation for previously unhedgeable event uncertainty (VERIFIED via search summary). — [AFA](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=260400)
- Dissenting/nuanced: an Applied Economics (2024) article "The pre-FOMC announcement drift: short-lived or long-lasting? Evidence from financial and volatility markets" exists, and a practitioner blog claims "The Pre-FOMC Drift is Alive"; neither could be read in full (403 / non-peer-reviewed). — [Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00036846.2024.2322573); [Quantseeker blog](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/trading-the-fed-the-pre-fomc-drift)
- Savor & Wilson (2013, Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis 48(2):343-375): 1958-2009 average excess return on days with scheduled inflation, unemployment or FOMC-rate news = 11.4 bp vs 1.1 bp on other days; >60% of the cumulative annual equity premium earned on announcement days; Sharpe ratio ~10x higher on announcement days (VERIFIED). — [IDEAS/RePEc JFQA](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v48y2013i02p343-375_00.html)
- Follow-up theory/evidence on the announcement premium: Ai et al., NBER WP 31923 "Macroeconomic Announcement Premium"; NBER WP 24432. — [NBER w31923](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31923/w31923.pdf); [NBER w24432](https://img.nber.org/papers/w24432)
- An "FOMC announcement reversal" paper also exists (title found; content not verified). — [ResearchGate: The FOMC Announcement Reversal](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362775066_The_FOMC_Announcement_Reversal)

### Inferences
- Classic post-publication decay: a ~0.5%/event edge with 8 events/year was arbitraged/hedged away within a few years of the 2011-2015 publication. Do not build on it; at most, FOMC proximity is a volatility/risk feature (widen stops, reduce size), relevant to FTMO daily-loss limits.
- The announcement premium is compensation for risk on those days, so it comes with jump risk — a poor fit for a daily -5% wall unless sized very small.

### Gaps
- No verified post-2019 (2020-2026) estimate of the pre-FOMC drift in index futures/CFDs; the 2024 Applied Economics paper's conclusions could not be read.
- No verified evidence that the announcement premium persisted after 2009 (sample end of Savor & Wilson).

## Q3. Macro-news effects on FX and bonds (Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold & Vega 2003) — speed of adjustment, drift or not, implications for 15-minute bars

### Takeaway
Scheduled US macro surprises cause near-instantaneous "conditional mean jumps" in dollar exchange rates, with adjustment essentially complete within the first 5-minute interval; bad news moves prices more than good news. There is no reliable post-announcement drift to harvest on 15-minute bars: the information is in the price before a 15-minute bar closes. The exploitable aspects are (a) risk management around releases (spread widening, jumps, slippage) and (b) the surprise itself, which is unknowable in advance.

### Cited Findings
- Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold & Vega (2003, American Economic Review 93(1):38-62), "Micro Effects of Macro Announcements: Real-Time Price Discovery in Foreign Exchange": six years of real-time quotes, expectations and realizations; announcement surprises produce conditional mean jumps; news-timing and sign effects; asymmetric reaction (bad news has greater impact than good news) (VERIFIED). — [NBER w8959](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w8959/w8959.pdf); [Kellogg page](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2003/micro-effects-of-macro-accouncements-real-time-price-discovery/); [SSRN 313660](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=313660)
- (FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE — NOT RE-VERIFIED) ABDV use 5-minute returns for DEM, JPY, GBP, CHF and EUR vs USD, Jan 1992-Dec 1998, and show the conditional mean adjusts within the 5-minute interval of the release ("quick" jump), while volatility adjusts more gradually; the same authors (ABDV 2007, Journal of International Economics) extend to stocks and bonds. — [NBER w8959](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w8959/w8959.pdf)
- EIA (oil) analogue showing the same pattern in commodities: 5-minute data show the weekly inventory release increases the odds of a jump at the announcement (10:35 Wednesday) by a factor of 42.2 and in 10:35-11:00 by 6.58 (VERIFIED, see Q8). — [Colorado State thesis (mountainscholar)](https://api.mountainscholar.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/9938aa1b-96a3-466b-ac76-7710737b5b72/content)

### Inferences
- On 15-minute bars, the release bar contains nearly the whole reaction; an AI that "sees" only closed M15 bars cannot trade the surprise and should not expect continuation in the following bars as a general rule.
- What can matter for COLLAPSAR: knowing that a scheduled release is inside the next bar (calendar feature) to avoid entering just before it or to account for gap/slippage risk through stops. CFD spreads widen sharply around releases (not quantified here).
- FTMO-specific caution: FTMO has rules about trading around high-impact news on some account types (UNVERIFIED in this session — must be checked against current FTMO terms before relying on any news-time strategy).

### Gaps
- No verified post-2010 replication of "no drift" on M15 for gold, indices, oil CFDs (some studies find small drifts in bonds/FX after certain releases, but none verified here).
- No numbers found on CFD spread widening around releases at FTMO's liquidity provider.

## Q4. Overnight vs intraday returns of equity indices (Cliff, Cooper & Gulen 2008; Kelly & Clark 2011; Lou, Polk & Skouras 2019) — relevance with weekday overnight holding allowed and weekends forbidden

### Takeaway
For US stocks/ETFs/index futures, essentially all the long-run equity return has been earned overnight (close-to-open) while regular-session returns are ~zero or negative; the pattern reportedly persisted through 2020-2024 in SPY. But this is mainly the equity risk premium (and overnight jump risk) relocated in time, not a free intraday edge; for a 24-hour CFD on index futures the "overnight" split is partly an artefact of the cash-market open/close. Long-only index exposure held overnight on weekdays (flat at weekends) earns the premium minus swap financing, which at retail CFD rates eats a meaningful part of it.

### Cited Findings
- Cliff, Cooper & Gulen (2008 working paper, "Return differences between trading and non-trading hours: like night and day"): across US stocks, ETFs and index futures 1993-2006, average overnight returns strongly positive, trading-hour returns near zero or negative; for individual S&P 500 stocks average overnight returns 0.028%-0.048% vs daytime -0.028% to 0.002% (VERIFIED via secondary summary). — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/buy-at-the-close-and-sell-at-the-open); [BSIC "The overnight puzzle"](https://bsic.it/the-overnight-puzzle/)
- Kelly & Clark (2011, Journal of Asset Management 12:132-145, "Returns in trading versus non-trading hours: The difference is day and night") reach the same conclusion on US index ETFs (FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE — NOT RE-VERIFIED). — [DOI 10.1057/jam.2011.2](https://doi.org/10.1057/jam.2011.2)
- Berkman, Koch, Tuttle & Zhang (2012, JFQA) link part of the pattern to attention-driven retail buying at the open that reverses intraday (VERIFIED via secondary summary). — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/buy-at-the-close-and-sell-at-the-open)
- Lou, Polk & Skouras (2019, Journal of Financial Economics), "A tug of war: overnight versus intraday expected returns": all abnormal momentum (and reversal) profits accrue overnight; other strategy profits (among 14) accrue intraday, typically with opposite-sign profits in the other leg; patterns robust, stronger for large-cap/high-price stocks; institutions prefer to trade intraday against momentum; time-variation in the overnight-intraday spread of ~2% per month (VERIFIED). — [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/87481); [Paper PDF](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf)
- Persistence: a practitioner study of SPY 2000-May 2024 finds overnight returns dominate and the pattern persisted after 2020; it also notes that the largest losses occur overnight too (risk premium for holding when market is closed); costs not analysed (VERIFIED, non-peer-reviewed). — [Robot Wealth](https://robotwealth.com/revisiting-overnight-vs-intraday-equity-returns/)
- The same source cites another study claiming nearly 100% of equity index futures returns came in one hour (2-3 am ET), flagged by the author as needing further investigation (UNVERIFIED claim). — [Robot Wealth](https://robotwealth.com/revisiting-overnight-vs-intraday-equity-returns/)
- Caveat that results depend on sample, market and definition of the open (VERIFIED). — [LuxAlgo summary](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/overnight-vs-intraday-returns/)

### Inferences
- Lou-Polk-Skouras is cross-sectional (stock-level strategies); COLLAPSAR trades indices, so the directly relevant piece is the index-level overnight premium, not the momentum split.
- With weekend flat rule, the long holds would capture Mon-Fri overnights but forgo Friday-close-to-Monday-open (historically part of the "overnight" return). Weekday swaps on index CFDs (long pays financing ≈ benchmark rate + broker markup) apply each night, often tripled on one night (see Q6). Net expected value of "hold index long overnight" is therefore the equity premium per night minus ~ (rate + markup)/365 per night — positive in low-rate regimes, much thinner in 2023-2026 rate regimes.
- Retail-media distortion: "buy the close, sell the open" is often promoted as a free lunch; it is a risk premium with overnight gap risk, and it ignores financing and spread on two round-trips per day.

### Gaps
- No verified study of overnight vs intraday split for DAX/other index CFDs that trade nearly 24h, or for gold/oil.
- No verified quantification of the post-publication (post-2011) size of the US overnight premium net of costs.

## Q5. FX intraday / time-of-day patterns: session effects (Breedon & Ranaldo 2013) and the London 4pm fix (Evans 2018; Melvin & Prins 2015; Krohn, Mueller & Whelan 2024)

### Takeaway
Two robust, mechanism-backed intraday FX regularities: (1) currencies tend to depreciate during their own local trading hours (order-flow driven; EUR/USD falls in the European morning and rises in US hours); (2) the US dollar appreciates into major fixings (Tokyo, ECB, London 4pm WM/R) and depreciates afterwards — a W-shaped daily pattern across nine major currencies over 21 years (Journal of Finance 2024). Month-end equity-hedge rebalancing at the 4pm fix adds a predictable, temporary move. Effects are small (basis points) and need very low costs; on retail CFD spreads they are borderline, but as features they fit 15-minute bars well.

### Cited Findings
- Breedon & Ranaldo (SNB Working Paper 2011-04; published as "Intraday patterns in FX returns and order flow", Journal of Money, Credit and Banking 2013): 10 years of high-frequency data; currencies tend to depreciate during local trading hours; EUR/USD tends to depreciate in the European morning and appreciate in US hours and "can form a simple, profitable trading strategy"; pattern present in order flow, linked to participants being net buyers of foreign currency in their own hours (VERIFIED for the WP abstract; JMCB citation 45(5):953-965 FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE). — [SNB WP 2011-04](https://www.snb.ch/en/publications/research/working-papers/2011/working_paper_2011_04); [QMUL WP 694](https://qmul.ac.uk/sef/research/workingpapers/2012/items/694.html)
- Krohn, Mueller & Whelan (2024, Journal of Finance 79(1):541-578), "Foreign Exchange Fixings and Returns around the Clock": the USD appreciates in the run-up to FX fixes and depreciates thereafter, a W-shaped intraday return pattern; 21 years, top nine currencies; reversals "pervasive and highly statistically significant"; inventory-risk explanation (dealers intermediating unconditional USD demand at fixes); natural experiments show timing is set by the published fixing (VERIFIED). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v79y2024i1p541-578.html); [INSEAD working version](https://sites.insead.edu/facultyresearch/research/file.cfm?fid=66802)
- Melvin & Prins ("Equity hedging and exchange rates at the London 4 p.m. fix"; published Journal of Financial Markets 2015 — journal details FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE): passive hedges of global equity portfolios are traded at the 4pm fix; relative equity-market appreciation over the month predicts depreciation of that market's currency before the final London fix of the month, followed by partial reversal (VERIFIED from abstract summary). — [ECB workshop version](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/events/pdf/conferences/131216/Third_FX_Workshop_MELVIN_PRINS_Equity%20hedging%20and%20exchange%20rates%20Nov%202013.pdf?37cad5300a1a2b823aec4ad71d721cec=); [SSRN 2019274](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2019274)
- Evans (2018, "Forex trading and the WMR Fix", Journal of Banking & Finance — journal details FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE) studies price behaviour around the fix; Ito & Yamada (NBER w21518, "Was the forex fixing fixed?") study fix-related price spikes (VERIFIED existence; detailed numbers not retrieved). — [MPRA 81583 (Evans)](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/81583/1/MPRA_paper_81583.pdf); [NBER w21518](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21518/w21518.pdf)
- Post-2013/2015 regime change: after the 2013-2015 fix-manipulation scandal, the WM/R fix window was widened from 1 to 5 minutes (FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE — NOT RE-VERIFIED); the 2024 JF paper's 21-year sample implies the dollar-around-fix pattern survived at least into its sample end.
- Retail-media distortion: the month-end 4pm fix trade is popularised on Substack/blogs as a reliable recipe (e.g., "The 4 PM London Fix Anomaly at Month-End"), often without costs or out-of-sample tests. — [Forex Detox Substack](https://forexdetox.substack.com/p/the-4-pm-london-fix-anomaly-at-month)

### Inferences
- Fits 15-minute bars directly: features "time to next fixing (Tokyo 9:55 JST, ECB 14:15 CET, London 16:00)", "local session of each currency", "month-end + relative equity performance of the month" are legitimate raw information for COLLAPSAR without becoming rules.
- Magnitudes are a few basis points per day per pair (exact sizes not verified here); with FTMO FX costs (spread + commission ~ $3/lot per side reported by a secondary source, see Q6) a pure fix-reversal trade on majors is likely marginal; it may help as timing for entries/exits of trades taken for other reasons.

### Gaps
- Exact basis-point size and post-cost Sharpe of the W-pattern and of Breedon-Ranaldo's EUR/USD strategy not retrieved.
- No verified evidence on whether gold (London PM fix/LBMA auction) shows the same fix-reversal pattern.

## Q6. Currency carry trade (Lustig & Verdelhan 2007; Lustig, Roussanov & Verdelhan 2011; Brunnermeier, Nagel & Pedersen 2008; Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen & Vrugt 2018) — performance since 2008 and retail CFD swaps

### Takeaway
Carry (long high-yield, short low-yield currencies) is the best-documented FX risk premium, with high pre-2008 Sharpe ratios and crash risk (negative skew, losses when volatility/funding stress spike). Since 2008 it was weak for over a decade (G10 basket ~0.7%/yr on ~10% vol over 2006-2023), then rebounded strongly in 2024-2025. For a retail/prop-firm CFD trader, carry is captured through daily swaps, which brokers mark up and which (at FTMO) cannot be avoided; combined with weekend flat rules (losing Friday→Monday is irrelevant since the triple-swap is booked Wednesday), the net carry of a typical G10 pair is reduced substantially, and crash risk collides with the -5%/day and -10% walls.

### Cited Findings
- Lustig & Verdelhan (2007, AER 97(1):89-117, "The Cross Section of Foreign Currency Risk Premia and Consumption Growth Risk"): portfolios sorted on interest-rate differentials show high-yield currencies earn higher excess returns, explained as compensation for consumption-growth risk (FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE — NOT RE-VERIFIED). — [DOI 10.1257/aer.97.1.89](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.97.1.89)
- Lustig, Roussanov & Verdelhan (2011, RFS 24(11):3731-3777, "Common Risk Factors in Currency Markets"): a slope factor (HML_FX: high- minus low-interest-rate portfolios) and a dollar factor explain most cross-sectional variation in currency excess returns (FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE — NOT RE-VERIFIED). — [DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhr068](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhr068)
- Brunnermeier, Nagel & Pedersen (2008, NBER Macroeconomics Annual 23:313-347, "Carry Trades and Currency Crashes"): carry returns are negatively skewed; high interest-rate differentials predict crash risk; crashes coincide with rising VIX/funding-liquidity stress and carry unwinds ("go up by the stairs, down by the elevator") (FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE — NOT RE-VERIFIED). — [NBER chapter c7286 via DOI 10.1086/593088](https://doi.org/10.1086/593088)
- Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen & Vrugt (2018, Journal of Financial Economics 127(2):197-225, "Carry"): carry predicts returns in global equities, bonds, commodities, currencies, credit and options; a diversified cross-asset carry portfolio has a higher Sharpe than any single asset class; carry strategies suffer in global recessions (FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE — NOT RE-VERIFIED; exact Sharpe figures not re-checked). — [DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2017.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2017.11.002)
- Post-2008 decline: "Currency carry trade: The decline in performance after the 2008 Global Financial Crisis" (Journal of International Financial Markets, Institutions & Money vol. 76, 2022, DOI 10.1016/j.intfin.2021.101460): carry earned higher returns and Sharpe ratios than US equities for decades before 2007 but has not been very profitable since the GFC, significantly underperforming the market in the decade after; for a leveraged carry portfolio with equity-like volatility the pre/post-GFC drop amounts to 5.4% per year (VERIFIED from abstract snippet; authors not retrieved). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfin/v76y2022ics1042443121001670.html); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1042443121001670)
- Long-run practitioner figure: a G10 carry basket returned 0.74% annualised on 9.8% volatility over ~19.5 years (2006-2023) (VERIFIED as reported by the blog; non-peer-reviewed). — [Alpha in Academia: "Carry's Zero"](https://www.alphainacademia.com/p/carrys-zero)
- Recent rebound: Bloomberg's 8-currency dollar-funded carry index gained ~18% in 2025, best year since 2009 (Hedgeweek) and similar strength in 2024 reported elsewhere (VERIFIED as press claims; the two sources' year attributions partly conflict, so treat dates cautiously). — [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/hedge-funds-pile-into-currency-carry-trades-as-returns-hit-two-decade-high/); [Finimize (Goldman view)](https://finimize.substack.com/p/goldman-says-carry-trades-havent)
- Swap mechanics at retail brokers: overnight swap depends on the interest-rate differential, size, direction, nights held and the broker's markup (brokers do not pass the full interbank differential); triple swap booked on Wednesday rollover to cover the weekend (VERIFIED, broker/blog sources). — [NYC Servers explainer](https://newyorkcityservers.com/blog/what-are-forex-swaps); [Tickmill FAQ](https://www.tickmill.com/about/faq/when-are-swaps-charged-at-a-triple-rate-for-currency-pairs-and-metals)
- FTMO specifics (secondary source, UNVERIFIED against FTMO's own pages): commission $3 per lot (per side) on FX, variable spreads, swap applies overnight with triple swap on Wednesday, no swap-free accounts. — [Propvator blog](https://propvator.com/blog/?p=2171)

### Inferences
- Carry is a multi-week/month premium; COLLAPSAR's horizon (≤ a few weeks) and weekend-flat rule mean carry would accrue Mon-Thu nights only (but Wednesday's triple swap still includes weekend interest — so the trader pays/earns weekend carry without bearing weekend gap risk; whether that is favourable depends on sign and markup).
- With markups, the long-carry side often receives much less than the true differential while the short-carry side pays more; on low-differential G10 pairs, net swap can be negative in both directions. The useful role of carry for COLLAPSAR is therefore (a) a cost term the AI must see (swap per night per instrument and direction) and (b) a slow information variable (rate differentials), not a standalone strategy.
- Crash risk (BNP 2008) is exactly what FTMO walls punish: one carry unwind day (e.g., August 2024 yen unwind — not verified in this session) can breach -5%/day if size is large.

### Gaps
- FTMO's actual swap tables per instrument (long/short points) were not retrieved; must be read from the MT5 symbol specification ("swap long/short") of the FTMO server.
- Authors of the 2022 JIFMIM "decline" paper not retrieved.
- No peer-reviewed estimate found of carry profitability net of retail CFD swap markups.

## Q7. Day-of-week and holiday effects — did they survive?

### Takeaway
The weekend/Monday effect (French 1980) and pre-holiday effect (Ariel 1990; Lakonishok & Smidt 1988) were strong in mid-20th-century US data but are widely reported to have weakened or reversed since the late 1980s-1990s; the S&P futures study cited in Q1 found TOM to be the only calendar effect still significant and persistent. Not a reliable edge; at most a weak feature.

### Cited Findings
- A study of S&P 500 index futures reports TOM as "the only calendar effect that is statistically and economically significant and persistent over time", implying day-of-week and holiday effects did not persist in that sample (VERIFIED snippet). — [Paperswithbacktest summary](https://paperswithbacktest.com/strategies/closing-the-question-on-the-continuation-of-turn-of-the-month-effects-evidence-from-the-s-p-index-futures-contract)
- (FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE — NOT RE-VERIFIED) French (1980, JFE 8(1):55-69, "Stock returns and the weekend effect") found negative average Monday returns on the S&P composite 1953-1977; Ariel (1990, Journal of Finance 45(5):1611-1626) found pre-holiday returns roughly 9-14 times the normal daily return; Lakonishok & Smidt (1988) also report pre-holiday and weekend effects. Later literature (e.g., Sullivan, Timmermann & White 2001, Journal of Econometrics, "Dangers of data mining: the case of calendar effects") argues most calendar effects are not significant once data-snooping across many tested rules is accounted for. — [French 1980 DOI 10.1016/0304-405X(80)90021-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(80)90021-5); [Ariel 1990 DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.1990.tb03731.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1990.tb03731.x); [Sullivan, Timmermann & White 2001 DOI 10.1016/S0304-4076(01)00069-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-4076(01)00069-X)
- Crypto day-of-week (non-peer-reviewed): Quantpedia's Bitcoin study finds intraday returns on NYSE-closed days (weekends/holidays) "stronger and more stable" than overnight returns, the opposite when NYSE is open (VERIFIED). — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/are-there-seasonal-intraday-or-overnight-anomalies-in-bitcoin/)

### Inferences
- The data-snooping critique is directly relevant to COLLAPSAR: an AI scanning many calendar features will find some that "work" in-sample; the project's hidden 12-month test and rotating exam are the right safeguards.
- Weekend-flat constraint makes Monday-open gap effects untradeable anyway.

### Gaps
- No verified post-2000 day-of-week evidence for FX, gold or oil found in this session.

## Q8. Commodity/oil seasonality and EIA weekly inventory announcements (brief)

### Takeaway
The EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report (Wednesday 10:30 ET) creates a predictable volatility and jump event in crude futures: prices react negatively to inventory builds above expectations, jumps are ~42x more likely in the release 5-minute interval, and there is evidence of pre-release informed trading. As with macro news, the move is concentrated in minutes — a timing/risk feature, not a drift to ride on M15. Oil seasonality was not verified in this session.

### Cited Findings
- 5-minute data: the inventory announcement raises the odds of jump arrival at the announcement (10:35 Wed) by a factor of 42.2 and in 10:35-11:00 by 6.58; increases annualised intraday volatility by 15.6% and trades by 2,104 in the same window (VERIFIED via search summary of the thesis). — [Colorado State / mountainscholar](https://api.mountainscholar.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/9938aa1b-96a3-466b-ac76-7710737b5b72/content)
- Futures returns fall with positive (bearish) inventory surprises and rise with negative surprises; strong announcement-day effect with prices moving in anticipation (VERIFIED summary). — [Hong & Luo, AUT working paper](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/29946/Hong-Luo-CLInventoryNews.pdf); [EIA presentation (Yang 2017)](https://www.eia.gov/finance/markets/reports_presentations/2017/yang.pdf)
- Suspicious pre-release order flow in WTI futures on days when inventories exceed forecasts: more buyer-initiated orders in the 2 hours before release with an average price drop of -0.25% (VERIFIED summary; paper "Informed Trading in the WTI Oil Futures Market"). — [HAL 02024317](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02024317)

### Inferences
- For oil CFDs on M15: the 10:30 ET Wednesday bar (and API Tuesday 16:30 ET, not verified here) are high-risk bars; the AI should know their timing. The -0.25% pre-release drift is a small, possibly informed-trading-driven signal; too small to verify as tradeable after CFD spreads.
- Note that FTMO's oil CFDs are futures-based with rolls (per project notes); roll dates are another calendar feature.

### Gaps
- No verified peer-reviewed evidence on oil seasonality (driving season, refinery maintenance) as a tradeable effect after costs.
- No verified post-2015 replication of the inventory-surprise effect sign/size.

## Q9. Crypto-specific calendar/time-of-day patterns and funding-rate effects

### Takeaway
Evidence on Bitcoin hour-of-day effects is mostly non-peer-reviewed: Quantpedia (2015-2022, Gemini data) finds the best returns at 21:00-23:00 UTC (when traditional markets are closed) and worst at 03:00-04:00 UTC, with a simple 21:00-23:00 long strategy reporting ~33-41% annualised before costs. Funding rates on perpetual futures transfer cash between longs and shorts every 8 hours and are a sentiment/crowding indicator; a theory paper on perpetual futures exists, but peer-reviewed evidence of a robust funding-rate trading edge was not found in this session. Crypto CFDs at retail brokers carry large swaps, which dominate any multi-day crypto carry/funding idea.

### Cited Findings
- Quantpedia (Padyšák & Vojtko, Feb 2022; own research, not peer-reviewed): Gemini hourly BTC data Oct 2015-Feb 2022 (UTC); returns at 22:00 and 23:00 most economically significant (~0.07% at 22:00); 03:00-04:00 worst; several positive hours significant at 5%, negative ones not; strategy buy 21:00 / sell 23:00: 33% annualised return, 20.93% vol, max drawdown -22.45%; no transaction costs assumed (VERIFIED). — [Quantpedia: Seasonal intraday or overnight anomalies in Bitcoin](https://quantpedia.com/are-there-seasonal-intraday-or-overnight-anomalies-in-bitcoin/); [Quantpedia: The Seasonality of Bitcoin](https://quantpedia.com/the-seasonality-of-bitcoin/?a=6080)
- A related summary reports 40.64% annualised and Calmar 1.79, with Friday the best day for the 21:00-23:00 window (VERIFIED as claimed by summary; numbers differ from the 33% figure above — different sample/specification; flag inconsistency). — [Quantpedia: The Seasonality of Bitcoin](https://quantpedia.com/the-seasonality-of-bitcoin/?a=6080)
- Other practitioner backtests of BTC intraday seasonality exist (Concretum Group; QuantifiedStrategies), non-peer-reviewed. — [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/seasonality-in-bitcoin-intraday-trend-trading/); [QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/bitcoin-intraday-seasonality-trading-strategy-backtest-results/)
- Funding-rate mechanics: computed every 8 hours on major exchanges; positive → longs pay shorts, negative → shorts pay longs; keeps perpetual price near spot (VERIFIED). Theory: He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter, "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures" (arXiv 2212.06888). — [arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06888); [MacroMicro funding-rate chart](https://en.macromicro.me/collections/3785/crypto/49213/bitcoin-perpetual-futures-funding-rate)
- (FROM PRIOR KNOWLEDGE — NOT RE-VERIFIED) Peer-reviewed intraday crypto work includes Eross, McGroarty, Urquhart & Wolfe (2019, Research in International Business and Finance, "The intraday dynamics of bitcoin") documenting intraday volume/volatility/return patterns tied to European and US trading hours. — [DOI 10.1016/j.ribaf.2019.01.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ribaf.2019.01.008)

### Inferences
- A 2-hour daily window yields many trades; on crypto CFDs with spreads of several basis points per round-trip, a ~0.07%/hour gross edge would be largely consumed — and it is from a sample that ended in 2022 with no out-of-sample test verified.
- CFD crypto at FTMO trades on weekends? (Project constraint says positions closed before weekends; crypto weekend hours would therefore be excluded — and the Quantpedia study says weekend intraday returns were the stronger part.)
- Funding rate is not paid on a crypto CFD; it is replaced by the broker's swap. Funding data could still be a sentiment input, but would require an external data feed.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed, post-2022 out-of-sample test of BTC hour-of-day effects found.
- No peer-reviewed evidence verified that funding rates predict BTC returns at 15-minute-to-weeks horizons net of costs.
- FTMO crypto CFD swap rates and weekend trading conditions not verified.
